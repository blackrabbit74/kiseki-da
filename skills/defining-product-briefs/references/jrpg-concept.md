# JRPG concept guide

Use this guide for a whole-game concept, not for an isolated character or scene.

Start with the player promise: who the player becomes, what choices they repeatedly make, and what emotional experience the game offers. Distinguish the creator's accepted premise from proposed elaboration.

Connect these layers in the brief:

- **Identity:** premise, theme, target player, tone, differentiating experience, and boundaries set by the creator.
- **Playable loop:** exploration situation → information or resource → meaningful choice → encounter or consequence → growth → changed possibilities. Describe one concrete cycle before listing systems.
- **Narrative engine:** protagonist motivation, party tensions, world pressure, central conflict, and the connection between chapter changes and player actions.
- **System pillars:** battle decision, progression tradeoff, exploration reward, and how each expresses the desired experience. Name the cost or lost opportunity that makes each choice meaningful.
- **Production envelope:** assumed platform, session length, content scale, team, and constraints. Keep unknown quantities as assumptions; do not invent a feasible schedule from feature count.
- **Concept proof:** one short playable or paper sequence that exercises the distinctive promise, its observation method, and a result that would warrant redesign.

For a broad seed, propose a coherent first concept rather than questioning every layer. Ask only about a missing choice that would redirect the whole game. Preserve unusual constraints instead of automatically adding genre conventions such as grinding, random battles, or a world-ending antagonist.

Finish with the smallest initial scope and the creative or production assumption that could invalidate it. Treat a concept brief as a design hypothesis; fun, accessibility, balance, and production feasibility need their own observations.
