# Defining Product Briefs

## Purpose

Describe what a product must achieve and how its concept will be judged.

## Deliverable

Return a concept brief or requested PRD with audience, problem or player promise, differentiation, core experience, capabilities, scope, success measures, risks, and open decisions.

Done when: each core capability serves a stated outcome and has an observable acceptance example; assumptions are visible.

Stop and report when: the core audience or intended experience cannot be inferred responsibly; identify the missing choice.

## Inputs

Use the seed, existing brief, user decisions, research, budget, platform, and production constraints when supplied.

## Decision rules

- If the user requests a quick draft, draft from stated assumptions rather than imposing a discovery workshop.
- If revising, preserve settled decisions and show the effect of requested changes.
- If a capability lacks a beneficiary or outcome, remove it or explain the unresolved rationale.
- For a JRPG concept, read [the JRPG concept guide](references/jrpg-concept.md).

## Required procedure

1. Establish the central promise and a representative user or player session.
2. Derive necessary capabilities and observable success from that session.
3. Separate initial scope, later possibilities, and the assumptions most likely to overturn the concept.

## Constraints (set by: operator)

- Do not turn proposed metrics, features, or production estimates into established facts. Instead, show their evidential status.
