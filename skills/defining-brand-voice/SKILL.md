# Defining Brand Voice

## Purpose

Turn identity claims into choices another writer can apply consistently.

## Deliverable

Return audience, purpose, positioning, differentiating evidence, voice principles, contrasting sample wording, and unresolved stakeholder differences.

Done when: voice principles produce distinguishable writing choices and positioning claims have examples or explicit assumptions.

Stop and report when: stakeholder accounts conflict on a central identity choice; preserve the alternatives and identify the owner of the decision.

## Inputs

Use existing copy, customer language, stakeholder statements, brand references, and intended medium.

## Decision rules

- If an identity word is generic, ask for a concrete example and a counterexample.
- If stakeholders disagree, keep their evidence separate before proposing a synthesis.
- If adapting voice to a medium, retain identity while changing length and degree of formality.
- If the user delegates drafting, propose a coherent voice instead of requiring a multi-session interview.

## Required procedure

1. Extract identity signals from actual language and behavior.
2. Form a positioning statement and a small set of usable voice choices.
3. Demonstrate the choices with paired examples and show unresolved claims.

## Constraints (set by: operator)

- Do not turn an aspirational brand claim into a proven customer outcome. Instead, distinguish desired identity from observed evidence.

## Model notes

- Claude: Identify targets by their decision criteria without redundant private labels.
- GPT: State boundaries once and honor the user’s current scope and choices.
