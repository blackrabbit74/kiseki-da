# JRPG player experience

Begin with one representative session and the information available to the player. Specify input method and platform when they change the interaction.

For an exploration decision, describe what invites attention, how the player forms a hypothesis, what action they can try, the cost or risk, and the discovery or consequence. Distinguish navigation friction from intentional uncertainty.

For a battle decision, show the visible state, available actions, their opportunity costs, enemy signals, and feedback. A decision needs a credible alternative; an action that dominates every situation does not create the intended tradeoff. Describe one encounter that exercises the concept without claiming it proves overall balance.

For progression, explain what the player chooses between, what becomes possible, whether the choice is reversible, and what happens if the player avoids optional grinding. Match recovery and retry costs to the intended experience.

Connect the loops: exploration changes information or resources, encounters change opportunities or relationships, growth changes the next exploration. Explain the connection through a concrete example instead of merely naming three systems.

For story interfaces, consider party knowledge, journal updates, objective clarity, spoiler control, and dialogue choices. Indicate what the player can inspect again after a break.

Check readability, input alternatives, save and retry behavior, interruption, and failure feedback at the conceptual level. Separate a design intention from evidence of accessibility or enjoyment. Finish with the specific observation needed from a paper walkthrough or playtest.
