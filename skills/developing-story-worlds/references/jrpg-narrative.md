# Narrative connected to JRPG play

Use this guide when outlining chapters or relating the story to a game-spanning experience. Respect the creator's desired balance between story and systems.

Describe each important chapter through a causal chain: present pressure → party objective → player action → consequential choice → changed world or relationship → new pressure. A cutscene can convey an event, but do not substitute it for every meaningful player action.

For each chapter, record the narrative purpose, explorable situation, meaningful encounter or noncombat challenge, new information, relationship change, and consequence carried forward. Include only fields relevant to that chapter; quiet recovery and exploration can matter without a boss battle.

Track the knowledge state of the player and party separately. A mystery needs a fair clue, a plausible current interpretation, a later reveal, and a changed understanding or choice. Maintain a reveal ledger when several secrets interact.

Keep agency legible. Distinguish a choice that changes the ending, a choice that changes a relationship or route, and a choice that expresses character without branching. Do not promise branching consequences the outline does not support.

Let side stories reinforce or challenge the main theme while remaining optional when promised. If an optional quest contains essential comprehension or progression, relocate that information or disclose the dependency.

For pacing, vary pressure, exploration, recovery, and connection according to the intended experience. Test a representative sequence rather than claiming the whole game will be engaging. A chapter outline is a proposal; actual duration, difficulty, and emotional impact remain untested until observed.
