# Characters and party relationships

Preserve the creator's established identities, relationships, and intended arcs. A new suggestion is a proposal, not a recovered fact.

For each major character, connect desire, fear or conviction, present strategy, pressure, difficult choice, and consequence. Use a transformation arc only when the story needs one; a steadfast character can change the world or other people instead.

Build relationships as changing obligations, trust, friction, and dependence. Give party members reasons to stay together that survive disagreement. A role such as healer or rival is not a complete motivation.

For a JRPG party, relate a character's narrative choices to their contribution in play when useful. Keep combat function and personality separable; the creator may deliberately subvert a familiar role. If a bond unlocks an ability, explain the shared decision or experience that makes the connection legible.

Test a major beat with two questions: why does this character choose this now, and what meaningful alternative do they reject? If the answer is only because the plot requires it, change the pressure, information, or choice.

Distinguish information known by the creator, the audience, and the character. Record when a revelation makes a former choice understandable; avoid retroactively giving characters knowledge they did not possess.

For dialogue or a voice sample, ground the difference in attention, word choice, rhythm, and relationship rather than a repeated verbal gimmick. Produce only the requested degree of detail.
