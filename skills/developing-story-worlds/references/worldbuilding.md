# World rules and pressures

Start from the creator's premise and rules already established. Keep accepted canon, proposals, and unresolved alternatives distinguishable.

Define the unusual rule through observable consequences: who can use it, what it costs, what prevents unlimited use, who benefits, and who pays. Test an edge case that could trivialize the central conflict. Repair only the gap the user authorized; otherwise present compatible options.

Make factions pursue concrete resources, security, status, or beliefs. Give opposing groups a reason a member could sincerely endorse. Identify the dependency or scarcity that makes cooperation and conflict recur.

Connect geography to lived action. A location needs a reason to exist, a pressure on its inhabitants, and an experience the player or reader can encounter. Lore that changes no choice can remain optional background.

For chronology, trace cause and effect rather than only dates: event → changed institution or relationship → present consequence. Separate actual history, public belief, and secret truth when they differ. Track what each faction could know at the relevant time.

When integrating magic or technology, connect it to labor, travel, communication, health, and power only where those consequences affect the requested concept. Avoid encyclopedic expansion unrelated to the creative gap.

Conclude the requested section with its key rule, one concrete lived example, and any contradiction that still needs a decision. New world facts remain proposals until the creator adopts them.
