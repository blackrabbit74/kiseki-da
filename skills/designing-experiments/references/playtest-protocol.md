# Small playtest protocol

Tie the test to a disputed player-experience claim, such as whether visible enemy intent creates understandable tradeoffs. Test the mechanism rather than asking whether the game is fun.

Describe the target player, relevant prior experience, playable or paper materials, task, comparison condition if feasible, and the information each participant receives. A facilitator's explanation can hide an onboarding problem; record when help was given.

Choose observable evidence: time to understand a goal, available options noticed, decisions made, explanation of a choice, repeated confusion, recovery after failure, and voluntary continuation. Use stated preference as an additional measure, not a substitute for behavior.

State beforehand which observations would support the claim, contradict it, or leave it unresolved. Preserve the planned measures when results disappoint; label newly noticed patterns as exploratory.

Record session conditions and observations separately from interpretations. A participant's silence is not consent or comprehension. Ask neutral follow-up questions about what they expected and why they acted, without teaching the intended answer.

Report sample and recruitment limits. A few convenient participants can expose usability failures but do not establish population preference or retention. If no real participants are available, run a clearly labeled internal walkthrough and deliver the remaining human test as prepared, not completed.
