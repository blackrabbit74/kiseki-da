# Researching Markets

## Purpose

Make market evidence useful for a specific entry, positioning, or investment decision.

## Deliverable

Return market boundaries, customer alternatives, competitor comparison, dated evidence, sizing calculations when requested, downside cases, and decision implications.

Done when: material numbers have sources or explicit assumptions, comparisons use consistent definitions, and the conclusion includes contrary evidence.

Stop and report when: available data cannot support the requested sizing precision; report a bounded estimate or the missing inputs.

## Inputs

Use the business decision, segment, geography, period, competitor set, business model, and accessible primary sources.

## Decision rules

- If comparing offers, normalize customer segment, units, terms, and observation date.
- If a claim comes from a vendor, attribute it and corroborate decision-critical performance claims.
- If estimating market size, show a bottom-up calculation and sensitivity to its main assumptions.
- If a competitor or price is unknown, record unknown rather than zero.

## Required procedure

1. Define the relevant customer problem and alternatives, including current workarounds.
2. Collect comparable primary evidence and trace every consequential number.
3. Explain positioning gaps, adoption barriers, and what would overturn the market thesis.

## Constraints (set by: operator)

- Do not convert unsupported market estimates into precise forecasts. Instead, expose the calculation and plausible range.

- Claude: Identify targets by their decision criteria without redundant private labels.
